WHY I MADE THIS PAGE:

I am mad, and I am typing fast, so forgive the typos. Open any magazine at the checkout line. Women in their twenties are shown to us as the finished product, and everyone else is a before picture. A woman turns forty and the glossy pages tell her she is a problem to be fixed. A woman turns fifty and the TV simply stops showing her, unless she is selling something to make her look younger.

Nobody voted for this! No committee decided that a wrinkle is a crime and a gray hair is a confession. But everybody acts as if it were the law of the land. So I figured: if it acts like a law, let's treat it like one, and let's write a better one.

So welcome to H.R. 9128. It is imaginary, but my anger is real, and I hope you pick up the phone.

THE BILL:

H.R. 9128
105th CONGRESS, 1st SESSION
THE AGELESS BODIES ACT

Be it enacted by the Citizens of these United States, that:

  1. SEC. 1. TRUTH IN PICTURES. Any magazine cover or advertisement that has been airbrushed or digitally trimmed shall say so in plain type, right on the page.
  2. SEC. 2. EQUAL TIME ON THE AIR. Television shall show women of every age, in real jobs and real roles, in rough proportion to how many of us there actually are.
  3. SEC. 3. NO MORE "ANTI-AGING" CLAIMS. Nobody may sell a cream or a potion by suggesting that growing older is a disease.
  4. SEC. 4. HANDS OFF OUR YEARS. No woman shall be passed over for a job, a part or a promotion because of the number of candles on her cake.

THE HALL OF SHAME:

Practices I object to, in no particular order:

  • The airbrushed cover where nobody has a pore, a freckle or a laugh line.
  • The "anti-aging" ad that treats a birthday like an emergency.
  • The "before and after" picture that changes the lighting too.
  • The sitcom where every grandmother is a punchline and every wife is a decade younger than her husband.
  • The "You Don't Look Your Age!" compliment. What exactly is my age supposed to look like?
  • The help-wanted ad that whispers "recent graduate preferred."
